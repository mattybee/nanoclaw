# Income tax and take-home — Australia

**Baseline: FY2026–27 (1 July 2026 – 30 June 2027), Australian resident.**
Verify on the ATO before quoting. These rates exclude Medicare and HELP.

## Resident brackets (FY2026–27)

| Taxable income | Tax on this income |
| --- | --- |
| $0 – $18,200 | Nil |
| $18,201 – $45,000 | 15c per $1 over $18,200 |
| $45,001 – $135,000 | $4,020 + 30c per $1 over $45,000 |
| $135,001 – $190,000 | $31,020 + 37c per $1 over $135,000 |
| $190,001+ | $51,370 + 45c per $1 over $190,000 |

The 16% → 15% cut on the second bracket applied from 1 July 2026. A further
cut on that bracket is legislated from 1 July 2027 — do not mix years.

LITO (max $700) is irrelevant at this income.

## Medicare

- **Levy:** 2% of taxable income. Full levy applies well above the low-income
  shade-in (singles ~$28,011 / ~$35,014 in this baseline year).
- **Surcharge (MLS)** if no appropriate private **hospital** cover. Income
  for MLS includes taxable income, reportable fringe benefits, and net
  investment losses.

FY2026–27 singles (verify):

| Tier | Single income | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base | ≤ $105,000 | 0% |
| 1 | $105,001 – $123,000 | 1% |
| 2 | $123,001 – $164,000 | 1.25% |
| 3 | $164,001+ | 1.5% |

They hold **Australian Unity basic** cover, taken mainly to avoid MLS.
Treat surcharge as **$0** once it is confirmed as **hospital** cover
(not extras-only) and the **partner is on the policy**. Family MLS
thresholds still matter if cover lapses or excludes the spouse.

## HELP / HECS (FY2026–27 baseline)

Repayments use **repayment income** (taxable income plus some add-backs),
not take-home. From 2025–26 the system is **marginal**, except the top band
which is 10% of **total** repayment income.

| Repayment income | Repayment |
| --- | --- |
| $0 – $69,528 | Nil |
| $69,529 – $129,717 | 15c per $1 over $69,528 |
| $129,718 – $186,050 | $9,028 + 17c per $1 over $129,717 |
| $186,051+ | 10% of total repayment income |

At ~$170k salary they are in the third HELP band. Job + net rent + ETF
income can cross **$186,051**, where the rate becomes 10% of **total**
repayment income — a sharp step. Ask whether they have a HELP balance
before including it.

## Working at ~$180k job + investments (illustrative)

On **$180,000** employment income (base + bonus + overtime) only, no HELP,
no MLS, no deductions:

- Income tax ≈ $47,670 ($31,020 + 37% × $45,000)
- Medicare levy $3,600
- **Subtotal ≈ $51,270** (marginal **39%** including Medicare)

Then **add** net Sydney rent (no interest) and ETF distributions — that
is the year-end taxable income, not the payslip. MLS: they have Australian
Unity basic for this purpose — confirm hospital + partner, then $0
surcharge. Always recompute with their actuals.

Cross-check: https://paycalculator.com.au/

## PAYG vs year-end

Employer withholding is an estimate. Investment income, deductions, MLS,
and extra super change the refund or bill. Don't treat the payslip as the
tax return.
