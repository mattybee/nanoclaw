# Shares, ETFs, and crypto

Client: about **$200,000** in ASX ETFs (**VGE, VAS, NDQ, HYLD**) plus
about **0.22 BTC** and **0.8 ETH**. Broker and parcel cost bases are
unknown — ask before CGT.

## ETFs (held, not day-traded)

Distributions are assessable in the year they are paid/attributed, even
if reinvested (DRP). Each fund issues an annual tax statement / AMMA
(AMIT). Typical components:

- Dividends (franked / unfranked) — VAS is the Australian-share one, so
  franking credits matter
- Foreign income + foreign tax offsets (VGE, NDQ, often HYLD)
- AMIT cost-base adjustments (increase/decrease) — keep these or the
  CGT cost base drifts
- Capital gains distributed by the fund (can happen without selling)

At tax time: CommBank or broker interest is separate; ETF tax is the
issuer statement, not the 31 Dec holding value.

Selling units is a CGT event. Discount 50% if held > 12 months
(individual, current baseline). Parcel matching: FIFO unless they have
better records.

Don't treat the $200k market value as income.

## Crypto

ATO: crypto is a CGT asset, not currency. **Buying and holding is not a
tax event.** Taxable examples: sell to AUD, swap BTC→ETH, spend BTC on
goods, move into some DeFi products. Transfers between their own wallets
are generally not.

They must keep: date acquired, AUD cost (including fees), date disposed,
AUD proceeds. If they have no cost base, say so and send them to the
agent — don't guess a 2017 spot price.

Unrealised gain on 0.22 BTC / 0.8 ETH is not lodged until a CGT event.

## Car

**Mitsubishi Pajero Sport**, 2020, bought **12 Aug 2020** for **$49,995**
at 2,024 km. Private. A car like this is generally **CGT-exempt**. Don't
put it in a CGT worksheet if they sell it. Don't claim running costs
without a work-km log that excludes commuting.

## Interaction with the rest of the return

ETF income and net rental profit add to taxable income. MLS: they have
Australian Unity basic cover — confirm hospital + partner, then don't
add the surcharge.
