Client is **48**, target **retire ASAP / no later than 52** (~4 years).
Born ~1978 → **preservation age is 60**. Verify:
https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/super/withdrawing-and-using-your-super/when-you-can-access-your-super

This is general information, not financial-product advice.

## The constraint that dominates

Stopping work at 52 does **not** unlock super. Standard conditions of
release (retirement, TTR, reaching 60) all need preservation age.
Assume super is **unavailable from 52 to 60** unless a rare condition
of release applies (don't fish for those).

"Can I retire?" = **can non-super cash cover the household for ~8 years?**
Super is a 60+ problem.

## The next four PAYG years

They put **nothing extra** into super today. That is optional while
working; it is nearly impossible to concessional-contribute at scale
once PAYG stops.

While still employed, the usual lever is salary sacrifice up to the
concessional cap (SG on OTE first — overtime is usually **not** OTE).
That lowers tax now and builds the pile they can only touch at 60.
Do not present it as money they can spend at 52.

Also useful before they finish work: insurance inside super (may lapse
or get expensive after they leave), and whether unused concessional-cap
carry-forward exists. Super balance is known (~$400k).

## Property and CGT if they sell to fund early retirement

Cost base starts at **$385,000 on 23 Apr 2004**. They **lived there
until Dec 2019**, then rented. The **6-year absence** (income-producing)
ended **December 2025**. They do not own another home.

As at Sep 2026 the clock has **lapsed**. Only days after Dec 2025 sit in
the CGT net — small now, larger if they hold until 52. Model a 2026 sale
vs a 2030 sale before treating the unit as 52–60 spending money.

A December 2019 **day** is only for a lodgement worksheet.

## How to answer

1. State preservation age 60 in the first reply.
2. Split the plan: **years 52–60 (non-super, ~$400k super sits)** vs **60+**.
3. Don't imply TTR or "retired so I can take super" at 52.
4. One question if a missing fact blocks the cash-flow test (partner on
   the health policy).
