# Work-related deductions — IT employee

Only claim what was spent to earn **this** employment income, and what
they can substantiate. The ATO occupation guidance for IT workers is the
filter; this file is a prompt list, not a shopping list.

## Work from home (from 1 July 2024, still the method in this baseline)

**Fixed-rate: 70c per hour** worked from home. Covers energy, internet,
phone, stationery, and computer consumables. Requires a **record of actual
hours** for the whole year (timesheet, roster, diary) — not a rough
estimate.

Cannot *also* claim those covered items separately. Can still separately
claim:

- Decline in value of office furniture and technology
- Repairs to that equipment
- Cleaning of a dedicated office (actual method / occupancy — be strict)

**Actual cost** is allowed if they have records and apportion private use.
Usually more work; only worth it if it clearly beats 70c × hours.

If the employer pays a WFH allowance or provides the internet/phone, don't
double-claim.

## Common IT employee claims (apportion private use)

- Professional memberships (ACS and similar) if they relate to current work
- Overtime meal allowance (only if conditions in the award / ATO are met)
- Self-education that **maintains or improves** skills in the *current*
  role — not a new career
- Union / professional association fees
- Tools and equipment: immediate if ≤ $300 and used >50% for work;
  otherwise decline in value
- Home internet and mobile **only if not using the 70c method for those**

## Usually not

- The **Pajero Sport** (bought 12 Aug 2020, $49,995) — private car.
  Home to a regular workplace is not deductible. No logbook, no claim.
  Cars of this type are generally **CGT-exempt** if sold.
- Client entertainment, most travel between home and a regular office
- Conventional clothing
- Employer-provided kit they didn't pay for
- A new degree aimed at a different job

## Substantiation

Keep receipts / bank records **five years** after lodgement. For WFH,
keep the hours log with the return. No diary, no 70c claim.

## High-income pattern

At 37–45% marginal, a **real** $1,000 work expense saves $370–$450 plus
Medicare. Fake or unapportioned claims are not worth an ATO review. Prefer
a short, evidenced list over a kitchen-sink schedule.
